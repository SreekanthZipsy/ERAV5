The Attention Bill

Vanilla attention was not wrong — it was expensive. Everything after it is somebody looking at that bill and trying to pay less of it. Laid out by launch date, not by family, you can watch the field change its mind.

What the timeline actually shows

As a list, these look like interchangeable “attention upgrades.” As a timeline, they are a mood swing.

2017 wants exactness: every query scores every key, softmax makes them compete, multi-head buys diversity. Position is bolted on (sinusoids, then learned tables) so the permutation-invariant mixer can tell “bank₂” from “bank₂₀.”

2019–2020 looks at the receipt. Decode bandwidth invents MQA. Long documents invent sparse edges and sliding windows. Softmax itself gets accused: drop it, regroup the products, keep a fixed d×d state — linear attention. Exact access is traded for a bill that does not explode with n.

2021 tries to buy back what linearization lost (delta rule / fast weights) while the mainstream stays on softmax and instead fixes position: RoPE for relative geometry, ALiBi for train-short/test-long. The obsession shifts from “how do I mix?” to “how do I still mean something at length L I never trained?”

2022–2023 is infrastructure and length patches. FlashAttention pays less HBM without changing the math. GQA softens MQA’s quality hit. NTK-aware and YaRN stretch RoPE. Attention sinks admit that naive windows delete the numerical drain the model learned to use. The field wants length without re-paying full pretrain.

2024–2025 the memory bill returns with a vengeance — not because researchers forgot length, but because serving at long context made KV cache the product. MLA compresses what you store. NSA (then DSA) adds trained sparse selection on compressed history. Gated DeltaNet puts control back into fixed-size state. DroPE asks whether RoPE was only a training scaffold all along.

Once you see that oscillation — exactness → memory → length → memory again — you can guess what comes next: hybrids that keep a little exact softmax for the needles, a compressed or recurrent state for the haystack, and position schemes that do not hold the context window hostage. A mechanism that is right for a 2K chatbot and wrong for a 1M agent is not a bad mechanism. It is a trade on a dated bill.
